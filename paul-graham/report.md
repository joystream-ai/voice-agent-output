# Paul Graham: voice study

**A strong read of this voice.** The test rewrite opens on the change, asks and answers the reader's question, and ends on a short flat line, with every fact kept and nothing added. It used only a few of his sentence habits, and several of his rules rest on a minority of the essays.

Built from 20 essays on his personal blog (paulgraham.com), newest first, dates not recorded. 15 rules kept, 6 cut.

## What it learned
- **He asks the reader's question and answers it at once.** 18 of 20 essays do this: "What do the YC partners look for? It's surprisingly simple." ([post](https://paulgraham.com/prepare.html))
- **He argues that writing and reading are how ideas get made.** Seen in 7 of 20 essays: "You can't think well without writing well, and you can't write well without reading well." ([post](https://paulgraham.com/read.html))
- **Essays usually open on a plain statement and often end on a short, flat line.** 14 of 20 open on a claim, and 12 of 20 close on a final sentence shorter than the paragraph's average: "But people who want to have ideas can't afford to." ([post](https://paulgraham.com/read.html))
- **He explains with a physical picture instead of an abstraction.** 13 of 20 essays: "The persistent are like boats whose engines can't be throttled back." ([post](https://paulgraham.com/persistence.html))
- **He opens conclusions with "So" and sharpens claims with "In fact".** Both appear in 18 of 20 essays: "In fact, the correlation between superlinear returns and inequality is so strong that it yields another heuristic for finding work of this type" ([post](https://paulgraham.com/superlinear.html))

## The test
The same bland company announcement every module gets, rewritten by following the new profile.

**Before**
> We are pleased to announce an update to our quarterly planning process. Going forward, all teams will submit their priorities through a shared template by the second week of each quarter. This change is designed to improve visibility across departments and ensure that resources are allocated effectively. Leadership will review submissions and share a consolidated plan within ten business days. We understand that change can take time to adjust to, and we appreciate everyone's flexibility. If you have questions or feedback about the new process, please reach out to your manager or the operations team. Thank you for your continued commitment to our shared goals.

**After**
> The quarterly planning process is changing. Every team submits its priorities through a shared template by the second week of each quarter. Leadership reviews the submissions, and a consolidated plan follows within ten business days. So who do you ask? Your manager or the operations team.

It sounds like him in its opening on a plain statement, the question answered straight away, and the short flat last line. It did not use his "So" for a real conclusion or his "In fact", because the paragraph has no claim to sharpen and forcing them would have bent the facts. Every fact survived and nothing was added.

## What's thin
- Several rules rest on 5 to 9 essays out of 20, including his views on where ideas come from, on what makes startups succeed, and the "Whereas" and "Strangely enough" habits.
- All 20 essays come from one blog, so format conventions may be mixed in. Nothing is known about his talks, emails or social posts.
- Counts for openings and endings come from code; the tags for analogies, endnotes, "So" and "In fact" were assigned by reading, so treat them as close, not exact.
- More essays from other years would show whether the analogy and "enough" habits belong to this period or to all his work.
