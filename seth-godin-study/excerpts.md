# Seth Godin: excerpts
Ground truth for the Seth Godin voice study.

## E1 · S1 · blog
> It's definitely tempting. All at once. Once and done. Over with.
>
> Pay the money, grit your teeth, launch with verve and then move on.
>
> Except…
>
> it almost never works.

Source: https://seths.blog/2026/11/one-swell-foop-2/

Demonstrates: R6 (fragment sentences as standalone paragraphs — "All at once. Once and done. Over with."), R3 (iterative process over single decisive action), R5 (single-sentence paragraphs), R14 (opens declaratively, not with a question)

## E2 · S2 · blog
> "No pressure" is something that's showing up more and more in the pitches I'm seeing. Of course, there's no pressure! Just saying it implies that you wish there were.
>
> If you're asking for a favor, you're not serving and you're not selling. You're trading.

Source: https://seths.blog/2026/11/selling-to-friends/

Demonstrates: R12 (redefinition — "you're not serving and you're not selling. You're trading."), R2 (serving vs. trading), R8 (exclamation mark inside quoted/reported speech only), R6 ("You're trading." — fragment as standalone sentence)

## E3 · S3 · blog
> I don't know what you know. I don't want what you want, see what you see, believe what you believe.
>
> And that's okay.

Source: https://seths.blog/2026/11/practical-empathy/

Demonstrates: R6 ("And that's okay." — three-word standalone), R5 (single-sentence paragraph), R7 (connector-opening paragraph), tricolon rhythm without Oxford comma before final item

## E4 · S3 · blog

## E5 · S4 · blog
> And yet we do it all the time. Ornate explanations about the stock market, SEO or politics. Charts and graphs where we spend most of our time fitting a plot to a few dots, or amplifying the obscure to justify the theory.

Source: https://seths.blog/2026/11/confusion-about-causation/

Demonstrates: R7 (connector-opening paragraph "And yet"), R9 (no serial comma: "stock market, SEO or politics"), R1 (failure of theory), R11 (figurative compression: "fitting a plot to a few dots")

## E6 · S6 · blog
> "I can't do that," might often be more honestly reported as, "I haven't chosen to be able to do that."

Source: https://seths.blog/2026/11/ten-minutes-a-day/

Demonstrates: R12 (redefinition via reframing a common phrase), R4 (the stated reason is not the real reason — applied to self-talk), one-line takeaway ending

## E7 · S8 · blog
> But all we really know is that this person, on this day, in this situation, hearing the story the way we told it, said no.

Source: https://seths.blog/2026/11/learning-from-rejection/

Demonstrates: R7 (connector-opening: "But"), R4 (stated reasons for rejection are almost never the real ones), deliberate slowing through serial prepositional phrases
