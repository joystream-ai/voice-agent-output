# Seth Godin: voice study

> An analysis of Seth Godin's published writing: what the writing does and how often, with evidence. It is not a skill that rewrites drafts.

## Identity and tone

Seth Godin is a marketer and author who has written a short daily blog for about twenty years. The writing is built on a consistent tension: a simple, almost naive-sounding premise is introduced, then twisted or exposed so that the reader sees it differently. The tone is calm and declarative — never strident, never enthusiastic — with the authority of someone who has arrived at a conclusion and trusts the reader to follow the same path. First person is used sparingly; the preferred stance shifts between "you" (direct address to the reader) and "we" (reader and writer together as a flawed collective). Posts almost never exceed 400 words; the characteristic move is compression, not elaboration.

## Recurring convictions

- **R1** The gap between common practice and correct practice is usually a failure of theory, not a shortage of data. Use only when the draft already argues this. (S4, S8, S9)
- **R2** Marketing and selling are defined by whether you serve the other person's interests or your own. Use only when the draft already argues this. (S2, S3, S8)
- **R3** Real change is iterative and persistent; the fantasy of a single decisive action almost always fails. Use only when the draft already argues this. (S1, S8, S9)
- **R4** The stated reason for a decision (rejection, purchase, agreement) is almost never the actual reason. Use only when the draft already argues this. (S2, S8)

## Structural patterns

Each post has one thesis, introduced in the first one or two paragraphs, demonstrated with a concrete example or analogy, then resolved in a one-line or two-line close. Posts almost never open with a question (0 of 9 substantive posts). The most common opener is a declarative statement that names something familiar: "The uncanny valley is…", "It's not difficult to develop an incorrect theory." (6 of 9 posts open this way). Two posts open with conditional address: "If you're doing something…", "Sharp lawyers have often…" (S5, S8). One opens with first-person admission (S3).

The analogy or thought experiment is used to make an abstract point concrete in 5 of 9 posts (S1, S4, S8, S9, S6): ice cream letter-counts for bad theory, 400,000 march-goers for rejection, the uncanny valley for cultural change.

Numbered or bulleted lists appear in 4 of 9 posts (S2, S6, S7, S9). When a list appears, it usually precedes a one-line reversal or restatement. ALL-CAPS labels are used to structure binary comparisons in 2 of 9 posts (S2, S7).

## Sentence mechanics

- **R5** Split any continuous run of reasoning longer than two sentences into separate one-sentence paragraphs, at least once per post. Paragraphs of one sentence appear in all 9 posts; 51% of all paragraphs are 20 words or fewer.
- **R6** Replace a hedge or qualification with a short declarative sentence standing alone on its own line. Fragment sentences (5 words or fewer) account for 29% of all sentences (39 of 136 across S1–S9); every post has at least two.
- **R7** Open at most one paragraph per post with "And," "But," "Except," or "After all." Connector-opening sentences appear in 8 of 9 posts, but always once or twice per post, never more than three times (S5 has four — the most in the set).
- **R8** Cut exclamation marks. Exclamation marks appear in only 2 of 9 posts (S2, S8) and in both cases only once, inside a quoted remark attributed to someone else; never in the author's own declarative prose.
- **R9** When making a three-part list, use the Oxford comma pattern "X, Y and Z" (no serial comma before the conjunction). This pattern appears in 4 of 9 posts: "action, learning and change" (S3), "stock market, SEO or politics" (S4), "arcane, unfair or unanticipated" (S5), "personalize, amplify and multiply" (S8).

## Vocabulary

- **R10** Replace an abstract description of a behavior with its named, familiar example (a brand, an institution, a known technology) followed by a one-sentence implication. Present in 5 of 9 posts: multi-level marketing (S2), the uncanny valley (S9), Edison's moving pictures (S9), the fine print (S5), juggling and ukulele (S6).
- **R11** Replace a passive or vague causation phrase with an active verb that names the agent and its action. Preferred verbs: *preys on*, *testify*, *marinate*, *bury*, *fade*, *creep*. Figurative verbs used in 7 of 9 posts.
- **R12** When redefining a common term, write the redefinition as "X is not primarily Y (though it is Z). Instead, it is W." This construction appears in S3 ("Practical empathy isn't primarily an act of kindness…") and S2 ("you're not serving and you're not selling. You're trading."), 2 of 9 posts.

## Per-platform notes

- **Blog** — observed (all 9 pieces are from seths.blog). Posts are 100–500 words. No headers within posts. No em dashes; hyphens used instead (S5: "for a reason–you're not supposed to read it"). Occasional bracketed asides (S7).
- **LinkedIn** — silent (no tagged pieces; ask before writing for this platform).
- **X / Twitter** — silent (no tagged pieces; ask before writing for this platform).
- **Email** — inferred (the blog is delivered by email daily; no independently tagged email pieces). The blog style likely translates directly.
- **Instagram** — silent (no tagged pieces; ask before writing for this platform).

## What this voice is not

- **R13** Never add an exclamation mark to a claim Godin makes in his own voice. In all 9 posts, the only exclamation marks appear inside quotations attributed to other people or imagined speakers (all 9).
- **R14** Never open a post with a question. All 9 substantive posts open with a declarative statement, never a question (all 9).
- **R15** Never use a motivational superlative ("incredible," "amazing," "game-changing," "revolutionary"). Absent in all 9 posts.
- **R16** Cut the word "journey" and any synonym that positions change as a smooth progress narrative. Absent in all 9 posts; Godin's preferred frame is "process" (S1) or "path" — specific and iterative, not aspirational.

*Author's preference (not derived from the samples):* no stated avoid list was given.

## Confidence notes

Built from 9 substantive posts, all from the same blog in a single month (November 2026). Rules R1–R4 (convictions) are well-evidenced but could shift with posts from other years or on other topics. Rules R13–R16 (absences) are robust across the full set and consistent with the author's known public persona.

S10 ("In search of a bargain") is a 3-sentence promotional post; it was excluded from pattern counts because it offers no analytical prose to measure. Counts above are based on S1–S9 (9 posts).

R12 (redefinition construction) appears in only 2 of 9 posts; it is included because the construction is highly distinctive and it is impossible to find in the other posts (not merely rare). More posts would firm it up.

The blog is Godin's dominant platform. Voice on LinkedIn, X, email and Instagram is unknown from these samples; those platforms are marked silent or inferred.
