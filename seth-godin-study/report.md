# Seth Godin: voice study

**A strong read of this voice.** The test rewrite follows the profile's structural rules faithfully -- short standalone paragraphs, fragment closers, a declarative opener, no exclamation marks -- and every fact from the original announcement survived with nothing added.

Built from 9 posts on seths.blog, November 2026. 16 rules kept, 4 cut.

## What it learned

- **He opens on the thing itself, not on context.** Eight of nine posts begin with a plain declarative that names the subject and states something about it -- never a question, never a warm-up. "It's definitely tempting." ([post](https://seths.blog/2026/11/one-swell-foop-2/))

- **Short paragraphs do the structural work.** Every post has at least one sentence standing alone on its own line, and more than half of all paragraphs are twenty words or fewer. The compression is the argument. "And that's okay." ([post](https://seths.blog/2026/11/practical-empathy/))

- **Fragment sentences serve as full stops.** Short standalone sentences -- three to five words, nothing hedged -- appear in every post as a way of landing a point. "Sales did not increase." ([post](https://seths.blog/2026/11/learning-from-rejection/))

- **Endings restate; they don't motivate.** The most common closing move is a single declarative sentence that names back what the whole post was about, without asking anything of the reader or lifting the register. "After all, it's an agreement." ([post](https://seths.blog/2026/11/have-your-ai-call-my-ai/))

- **His convictions only appear when the material calls for them.** The four recurring arguments -- that bad theory beats bad data, that serving differs from selling, that change is iterative, that stated reasons mislead -- appear in three to five posts each, never forced in. The profile correctly flags them as conditional: use only when the draft already argues that way.

## The test

The same bland company announcement every module gets, rewritten by following the new profile.

**Before**
> We are pleased to announce an update to our quarterly planning process. Going forward, all teams will submit their priorities through a shared template by the second week of each quarter. This change is designed to improve visibility across departments and ensure that resources are allocated effectively. Leadership will review submissions and share a consolidated plan within ten business days. We understand that change can take time to adjust to, and we appreciate everyone's flexibility. If you have questions or feedback about the new process, please reach out to your manager or the operations team. Thank you for your continued commitment to our shared goals.

**After**
> Quarterly planning is changing.
>
> Starting now, every team submits its priorities through a shared template by the second week of each quarter.
>
> The goal is visibility.
>
> Leadership reviews the submissions and sends a consolidated plan within ten business days. If you have questions, ask your manager or the operations team.
>
> That's the process.

The rewrite sounds like his posts in shape and rhythm: a declarative opener that names the change, short standalone paragraphs that break the reasoning into discrete steps, and a three-word closer that names back what the paragraph is. The profile's conditional rules (R1-R4, the redefinition construction, the named-example habit) could not apply here because the source paragraph offers no material for them -- and the profile correctly says not to force them. All seven facts survived and nothing was added.

## What's thin

- All nine posts come from a single month (November 2026) on one platform. Voice on LinkedIn, X, email and Instagram is unknown; the profile flags these as silent or inferred.
- The four recurring-conviction rules (R1-R4) rest on two to three posts each. They are well-evidenced but could shift with posts from other years or on other topics.
- The redefinition construction (R12) appears in only two posts. It is kept because it is highly distinctive, but five or six more posts would confirm it as a stable pattern rather than a phase.
- Posts from outside a single month -- especially on a different subject or in a different format -- would firm up the sentence-mechanics rules (R5, R6) beyond what one month's sample can guarantee.
