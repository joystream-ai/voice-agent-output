# Morgan Housel: voice study

**A strong read of this voice.** The test rewrite kept every fact, added nothing, and followed the profile's habits for connecting sentences, endings and structure. It showed only a few of his distinctive habits, because the bland paragraph gives them little to work on.

Built from 19 posts on the Collaborative Fund blog. 18 rules kept, 7 cut.

## What it learned
- **He argues that forecasts are a known number multiplied by a story.** This appears in 6 of 19 posts: "Every forecast takes a number from today and multiplies it by a story about tomorrow." ([post](https://collabfund.com/blog/numbersandstories/))
- **Posts often open on a named person or event, then make the claim.** 9 of 19 posts do this: "A boy once asked Charlie Munger, “What advice do you have for someone like me to succeed in life?”" ([post](https://collabfund.com/blog/very-bad-advice/))
- **Some posts end on one plain line.** 7 of 19 close on a single sentence with no recap: "It’s hard to remember how you felt when you know how the story ends." ([post](https://collabfund.com/blog/a-message-from-the-past-thoughts-on-nostalgia/))
- **He starts a sentence with But or And where others would write however.** This is in 16 of 19 posts: "Most people can afford to not be a great investor. But they can’t afford to be a bad investor." ([post](https://collabfund.com/blog/little-rules-about-big-things/))
- **He writes "it's easy to" where others write that people fail to notice something.** 10 of 19 posts: "It’s easy to underestimate how social norms stall change, even when the change is an obvious improvement." ([post](https://collabfund.com/blog/a-few-short-stories/))

## The test
The same bland company announcement every module gets, rewritten by following the new profile.

**Before**
> We are pleased to announce an update to our quarterly planning process. Going forward, all teams will submit their priorities through a shared template by the second week of each quarter. This change is designed to improve visibility across departments and ensure that resources are allocated effectively. Leadership will review submissions and share a consolidated plan within ten business days. We understand that change can take time to adjust to, and we appreciate everyone's flexibility. If you have questions or feedback about the new process, please reach out to your manager or the operations team. Thank you for your continued commitment to our shared goals.

**After**
> Our quarterly planning process is changing.
>
> All teams will submit their priorities through a shared template by the second week of each quarter. Leadership will review submissions and share a consolidated plan within ten business days.
>
> But change can take time to adjust to, and we appreciate everyone's flexibility.
>
> Questions go to your manager or the operations team.

It opens flat on the change, uses a But opener in place of a stiff connector, has no subheadings, and ends on who to ask rather than a thank-you or a call to act. Most of his deeper habits (a named story, a semicolon reversal, "it's easy to") had nothing in the paragraph to attach to, and adding them would have invented content. Every fact survived and nothing was added.

## What's thin
- Several convictions rest on only four to six posts, and one rule about stories followed by a lesson rests on three.
- All 19 posts come from one company blog, and one is a podcast transcript, so some patterns may be that blog's conventions. Nothing covers LinkedIn, X, Instagram or email.
- Pattern counts were tagged by hand from reading, not measured by script. Sentence and paragraph lengths were not measured.
- The rewrite visibly used fewer than three of his distinctive habits, so on a plain announcement it reads close to a plain-English edit. More essay-form posts would firm up the sentence-level habits.
