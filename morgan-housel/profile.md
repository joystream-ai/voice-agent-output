# Morgan Housel: voice study

> An analysis of Morgan Housel's published writing: what the writing does and how often, with evidence. It is not a skill that rewrites drafts.

## Identity and tone
Partner at Collaborative Fund; writes essays about money, behaviour and history. In these 19 posts the voice is a calm, first-person essayist who reasons from named historical stories and short quoted lines, states conclusions as plain flat sentences, and admits uncertainty. Humour is dry and blunt rather than jokey.

## Recurring convictions
- **R1** Hindsight makes the past look easier and more certain than it felt, so nostalgia and confident forecasts both mislead; use only when the draft already argues this. This sometimes holds in 4 of 19 pieces (S2, S4, S11, S19). It is seen at most once per piece. Excerpts: E2.
- **R2** People want different things, so one-size-fits-all advice and money debates between different personalities go wrong; use only when the draft already argues this. This sometimes holds in 6 of 19 pieces (S3, S4, S9, S12, S15, S16). It is seen at most once per piece. Excerpts: E10.
- **R3** As life improves, expectations rise and complaints shrink to smaller problems instead of contentment arriving; use only when the draft already argues this. This sometimes holds in 6 of 19 pieces (S1, S2, S4, S6, S10, S17). It is seen at most once per piece. Excerpts: E7.
- **R4** A forecast is a known number multiplied by a story, and unsustainable things outlast expectations; use only when the draft already argues this. This sometimes holds in 6 of 19 pieces (S4, S5, S14, S17, S18, S19). It is seen at most once per piece. Excerpts: E11, E13.

## Structural patterns
- **R5** Open with a named person, event or dated fact and put the claim after it, instead of opening on the general claim. This sometimes holds in 9 of 19 pieces (S1, S6, S7, S10, S11, S15, S16, S17, S18). It is seen at most once per piece. Excerpts: E1, E3.
- **R6** Replace an abstract illustration with a named person or event from history, with a year or figure where the draft has one. This usually holds in 13 of 19 pieces (S1, S2, S4, S5, S6, S7, S10, S11, S14, S15, S16, S17, S18). Excerpts: E1, E11.
- **R7** Open with a single short line that introduces a list or run of points with a few, or put that in the title, and cut any longer preamble. This usually holds in 14 of 19 pieces (S2, S4, S5, S6, S7, S8, S9, S10, S12, S13, S14, S16, S17, S18). Excerpts: E4, E5.
- **R8** Merge a run of parallel points into paragraphs that each begin with a bold sentence stating the whole claim. This sometimes holds in 9 of 19 pieces (S2, S3, S7, S9, S10, S12, S13, S14, S16). It is seen at most once per piece. Excerpts: E12.
- **R9** Replace the transition between self-contained anecdotes with a horizontal break. This sometimes holds in 6 of 19 pieces (S1, S5, S11, S12, S17, S18). It is seen at most once per piece.
- **R10** End with one flat sentence after each short story that applies it beyond its subject, instead of ending on the quote or the facts. This usually holds in 3 of 3 pieces (S5, S17, S18). Excerpts: E11.
- **R11** End with a single plain sentence or short paragraph that states the lesson, after the last example, instead of a recap. This sometimes holds in 7 of 19 pieces (S1, S4, S9, S10, S11, S17, S18). It is seen at most once per piece. Excerpts: E2, E7.

Openers: 9 named person or event or fact, 4 one-line list introduction, 3 general claim, 2 announcement or context, 1 question.

## Sentence mechanics
- **R12** Merge a claim and its reversal into one sentence joined by a semicolon. This sometimes holds in 7 of 19 pieces (S1, S4, S6, S7, S9, S11, S15). It is seen at most once per piece. Excerpts: E6.
- **R13** Replace however, moreover and furthermore with a sentence that starts with But or And. This usually holds in 16 of 19 pieces (S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S7, S9, S10, S11, S12, S14, S15, S16, S17, S18, S19). Excerpts: E14.
- **R14** Keep a first-person hedge (I think, I bet) on claims about what people in general feel or do, and cut it from facts. This usually holds in 12 of 19 pieces (S1, S2, S3, S6, S9, S10, S11, S14, S15, S16, S18, S19). Excerpts: E15.

## Vocabulary
- **R15** Replace a statement that people fail to notice something with a sentence that starts it's easy to, plus underestimate, overlook, forget or mistake. This usually holds in 10 of 19 pieces (S2, S4, S7, S9, S10, S11, S12, S15, S17, S19). Excerpts: E16.
- **R16** Replace a polite euphemism with the plain blunt word when the point is bluntness. This sometimes holds in 5 of 19 pieces (S2, S4, S10, S11, S18). It is seen at most once per piece. Excerpts: E8, E9.

## Per-platform notes
- blog: observed. All 19 pieces are posts on one company blog (S19 is a podcast transcript published as a post).
- linkedin, x, instagram, email: silent. Ask before writing for these platforms.

## What this voice is not
- **R17** Cut a closing call to act (buy, subscribe, share, follow) from the end of an essay. This usually holds in 18 of 19 pieces (S1, S2, S4, S5, S6, S7, S8, S9, S10, S11, S12, S13, S14, S15, S16, S17, S18, S19).
- **R18** Cut subheadings inside a post and use bold lead-ins or horizontal breaks instead. This always holds in 19 of 19 pieces (S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6, S7, S8, S9, S10, S11, S12, S13, S14, S15, S16, S17, S18, S19).

## Confidence notes
- Built from 19 posts, all from one blog, so some patterns may be that blog's conventions.
- Strongest: R18 (all 19) and R13 (16 of 19). Thinner: R1 and R10, each seen in few pieces.
- Pattern counts come from a per-piece tag table that I filled in by reading each post and a script then tallied; sentence and paragraph length were not measured, so no rule states one. Treat the counts as hand-tagged, not machine-measured.
- Single-piece observations, not rules: S19 is spoken, long and repetitive in a way no other piece is; S3 is a book announcement.
- Posts that are lists of aphorisms or questions (S4, S6, S8, S13) show little sentence-level voice. More essay-form posts would firm up R12 to R15.
- Fewer than the ideal 20 posts were usable; one link post was skipped.
